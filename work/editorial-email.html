The Photo Mentorship · 2026 · Email

An editorial email that stays editable

“There Are No Magic Settings” invites subscribers to a free bird photography lesson. I designed it to feel like a magazine page, then wrote a build spec so it could be made entirely from ActiveCampaign’s drag-and-drop blocks. Anyone on the team can change it later without touching code.

My part
Email design, mobile layout, row-by-row build specification
With
The TPM team (approval); lesson by Tibor Molnar
Platform
ActiveCampaign

Context

The Photo Mentorship sends regular emails that point subscribers to new free lessons. This one promotes a bird photography lesson in which Tibor Molnar explains shutter speed, ISO, aperture, and autofocus. Campaigns are built and scheduled in ActiveCampaign, which is part of my regular work.

The need

  • A more considered, editorial look than a standard newsletter template.
  • No custom HTML. Coded templates are hard for non-developers to edit, and small changes become risky.
  • A layout that holds up on phones, where many subscribers read.

What I did

I designed the email at 600 px for desktop and 390 px for mobile. Then I wrote a build spec that describes every row in the order it is built: which native block to use, its content, spacing, colours, and font, with fallbacks for email clients that don’t load web fonts.

Build method
Native ActiveCampaign blocks only
Structure
Single-column rows
Headings
Playfair Display → Georgia
Body
Barlow → Helvetica, Arial
Side padding
64 px desktop, 24 px mobile
Corners
Square everywhere, including the button

The spec also fixes the palette, built on TPM’s brand cyan and navy:

  • Cyan #00CCFF
  • Navy #12285C
  • Paper #FBFAF8
  • Warm grey #F1EEE8
  • Brass #C69A6A
  • Body text #3D4457

Decisions

Editable beats clever

Every element (headline, button, sign-off, social icons) is a separate native block, so the team can click and change it in ActiveCampaign’s editor. That ruled out multi-column tricks and layered images.

Type does the work

A serif headline, a thin brass rule, and generous spacing give the email its look. That style survives every email client, unlike background images or custom code.

One clear action

The email has a single button, “Show me the settings,” in navy with a cyan top edge, directly after the copy that explains what the lesson covers.
