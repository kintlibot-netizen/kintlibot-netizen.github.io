The Photo Mentorship · 2026 · YouTube & blog

A thumbnail system for photography lessons

The Photo Mentorship teaches photography through free lessons on YouTube and its blog. I design the thumbnails and in-article graphics, so a lesson on window light, family posing, or wildlife settings is recognisable at a glance and clearly part of the same brand.

My part
Layout and type, headline variations, image selection, export
With
David Molnar and the TPM team: photography, lesson content, final selection
Output
4–6 options per lesson at 1920 × 1080

Context

Each free lesson needs a YouTube thumbnail and graphics for the matching blog post. They come in series: a block of lessons on window-light portraits, a Family Photography 101 course, a run of wildlife lessons. Most viewers see a thumbnail at a few hundred pixels wide, next to other photography channels using the same kind of imagery.

The need

  • Thumbnails that read at small sizes, with one short headline and a clear subject.
  • A look that changes with the topic but still reads as The Photo Mentorship.
  • Enough options per lesson for David to choose quickly, without a long back-and-forth.

What I did

For each lesson I pick photographs from the TPM library that show the lesson’s point, write short headline variations from the lesson content, and lay out four to six options. I keep the files in one system: same canvas, same margins, a small type style for the series name, and a headline set large enough to survive a phone screen.

Decisions

Let the topic set the type

Portrait and family lessons use a light editorial serif, which suits slower, people-focused subjects. Wildlife lessons switch to a heavy sans-serif, because those lessons are about speed and the headlines are instructions (“Set this first.”).

Never cover the subject

Text goes into the empty part of the frame: sky, snow, a plain wall. If a photo has no clear space, I choose a different photo rather than putting a box behind the headline.

Short enough to read on a phone

Headlines stay at about six words or fewer. A smaller line underneath can explain more on the blog, where the image is shown larger.

In-article graphics

Blog posts get wider graphics that teach one idea each, so a reader skimming the article still gets the main points.

Deliverables

  • Thumbnail option sets at 1920 × 1080 for each lesson in the window-light, family, and wildlife series
  • In-article teaching graphics for the matching blog posts
  • A consistent file and naming system, so options can be compared and picked quickly

Result

Complete option sets for the window-light, family, and wildlife series, each ready for the team to choose from. The same layout rules hold across very different subjects, from portraits by a window to wildlife in snow.
